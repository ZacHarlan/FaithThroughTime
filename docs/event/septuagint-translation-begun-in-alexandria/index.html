Septuagint Translation Begun in Alexandria

c. 277 BC

Tradition holds that at the request of Ptolemy II Philadelphus, seventy-two Jewish scholars from Jerusalem translated the Law into Greek at Alexandria. The Greek Old Testament that grew from this work, the Septuagint, became the Bible of Greek-speaking Jews and of most New Testament writers who quote Scripture.
