Great Schism: East-West Split

AD 1054

In July 1054 Cardinal Humbert, legate of Pope Leo IX (who had died that April), laid a bull of excommunication on the altar of Hagia Sophia, and Patriarch Michael Cerularius and his synod responded in kind. The episode became the conventional date for the division of Christendom into a Roman Catholic West and an Orthodox East, though the estrangement over papal authority, the filioque clause and diverging customs was gradual and long in the making.
