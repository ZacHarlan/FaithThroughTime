Person · Reformer

William Tyndale

c. AD 1494–1536

A gifted linguist who translated the New Testament into English directly from the Greek, printing it abroad and smuggling copies into England in bales of cloth. Strangled and burned as a heretic, his phrasing lives on, since much of the King James Version, above all its New Testament, descends from his work.
